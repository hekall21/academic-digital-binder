BAB 1 The Big Picture: Present-Past-Future Formula for Tech Professionals

Materi Slide Resmi

In international technical interviews, professional networking events (LinkedIn, GitHub), and academic symposiums, delivering a concise elevator pitch is essential. The industry-standard structure is the Present-Past-Future Formula:

The 3-Part Pitch Formula:
1. PRESENT (Current Status): Who you are right now and your current technical domain.
2. PAST (Foundation & Experience): Projects built, technologies mastered, or challenges overcome.
3. FUTURE (Vision & Goals): What you aim to contribute, solve, or learn next.

BAB 2 Script Model & Industry Dialogue Transcript

Materi Slide Resmi
Professional Profile Script Sample:

"Good morning, everyone. My name is Muhammad Haikal. I am currently an undergraduate student in Information Systems at Universitas Indraprasta PGRI (Unindra). [PRESENT]
Over the past year, I have built web-based digital archive tools and explored algorithm optimization using Pascal and modern JavaScript frameworks. [PAST]
Moving forward, I aim to specialize in scalable full-stack software architecture and cloud database systems to contribute effectively to high-impact enterprise solutions. [FUTURE]"

BAB 3 Grammar Foundation: Present Simple Mechanics & Subject-Verb Agreement

Materi Slide Resmi

The Present Simple is the workhorse of technical writing, used to describe general truths, system specifications, permanent states, and habitual routines:

Subject Category Affirmative (+) Negative (-) Interrogative (?) Engineering Example
I / You / We / They (Plural) S + Verb 1 + Object S + do not (don't) + V1 Do + S + V1? "We deploy microservices using Docker containers."
He / She / It (Singular) S + Verb 1 (s/es) + Object S + does not (doesn't) + V1 Does + S + V1? "The PostgreSQL database handles ACID transactions efficiently."

Rules for Adding -s / -es to Third-Person Singular Verbs:

  • Verbs ending in -ch, -sh, -ss, -x, -z, -o: add -es (watches, pushes, passes, fixes, goes).
  • Verbs ending in consonant + y: change y to i and add -es (queries, studies, modifies).
  • Verbs ending in vowel + y: simply add -s (plays, displays).

PENGAYAAN 1 Exam Traps & Technical Vocabulary (Adverbs of Frequency)

⚠️ Placement of Adverbs of Frequency:
Adverbs of frequency (always, usually, often, frequently, rarely, never) must be positioned:
  • BEFORE main verbs: "The caching server always responds in under 10 milliseconds."
  • AFTER auxiliary / to-be verbs: "The backup cron job is never skipped during maintenance."
